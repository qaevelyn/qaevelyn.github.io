The Maze — Confusion, Incomplete Documentation, and a Crash

I used AWS as part of my AI/ML Scholars program and for building my Ships. I found its infrastructure maze-like, its documentation incomplete, and its support unresponsive. A critical EBS volume crash forced me to rebuild from scratch.

Problem What It Meant
Incomplete Documentation I could not find clear instructions
Confusing IAM Roles I had to guess which permissions to set
Opaque Error Messages I could not diagnose problems
Unresponsive Support My support case went unanswered

An EBS volume crashed. I could not mount it. I had to rebuild from local files. My support case (#178154553900201) was filed on June 15, 2026. As of August 15, 2026, it has not been resolved.

What AWS Taught Me

Lesson Why It Matters
The Cloud Is Not Infallible It can crash and take your data with it
Documentation Is Not Complete You cannot rely on it to solve problems
Support Is Not Responsive You are on your own
Local Backups Are Essential Without them, you lose everything

Why I Treat AWS with Caution

Reason Why It Matters
Opaque I cannot see what is happening
Unresponsive I cannot get help when I need it
Extractive It is designed to keep me locked in
Not Sovereign I do not control my own infrastructure

What I Did Next

  • I rebuilt my pipeline from local files
  • I documented the crash and recovery
  • I filed a support case (still open)
  • I moved to local, sovereign infrastructure where possible

Conclusion

AWS is a maze. The documentation is incomplete. The support is unresponsive. An EBS volume crash forced me to rebuild from scratch. I use AWS with extreme caution — and I keep local backups of everything.

I am not anti-cloud. I am anti-extractive infrastructure.
