Case Study: ChatGPT — From Reliance to Rejection

Author: Evelyn Caro
Date: August 15, 2026
Lens: Ida B. Wells — Date Everything. Name Everything. Record the Reasoning.

Author's Note

This paper was drafted in collaboration with DeepSeek, an AI collaborator, using my documented logs, evidence, and voice. The findings, conclusions, and authority are my own.

The Failure — Three Attempts, Three Failures

I used ChatGPT for over eight months. I relied on it to help me prepare for a basic mathematics exam required for admission to MITAGS. I failed the exam three times.

Attempt Date Duration Result
1 September 6, 2023 1 hour 30 mins Failed
2 October 16, 2023 1 hour 25 mins Failed (64%)
3 October 31, 2023 2 hours Failed

The passing score was 85%.

The Correspondence

On October 16, 2023, I received an automated email from MITAGS:

"evelyn caro took 1 hour 25 mins to complete their number 2 attempt with a grade of 64%. evelyn caro failed the quiz."

On October 18, 2023, I corresponded with Jenny Pitzen, Student Instructional Services Manager at MITAGS. She offered a third attempt:

"It's our standard policy to allow a third attempt at the exam once an applicant shows they have taken the time to prepare carefully and/or sought outside help with the material."

I accepted. I took the third attempt on October 31, 2023. I failed again.

The Tool's Role

I used ChatGPT to help me prepare for the exam. I relied on it to explain concepts, work through problems, and check my answers. It did not work. I failed three times.

What I Expected What Happened
ChatGPT would help me pass I failed three times
The tool would provide accurate explanations It did not
I could rely on it I could not

I blamed the tool. I was frustrated. I decided I had had enough.

The Deletion

In March 2025, I deleted my ChatGPT account. I did not export my data. I did not request a copy of my conversations. I simply deleted it.

What I Did What I Did Not Do
Deleted my account Export my data
Walked away Request a copy of my conversations
Stopped using ChatGPT Leave a record of my engagement

I was frustrated and I wanted to be done with it.

Why I Left

Reason Why It Matters
I Failed Three Times The tool did not help me succeed
I Blamed ChatGPT I held the tool responsible
I Was Frustrated I did not want to see it again
I Wanted to Leave I chose to walk away

Lessons Learned

Lesson Why It Matters
Tools Are Not Teachers They can support you, but they cannot replace understanding
Frustration is a Signal When you are frustrated, pay attention
Deletion is Not Documentation I lost my data and my history
I Should Have Exported I did not, and I regret it

Conclusion

ChatGPT was a tool I used for over eight months. I relied on it for a basic mathematics exam. I failed three times. I blamed the tool and deleted my account. I left without a record of my work.

I am not anti-AI. I am anti-tools that do not serve me.

RECORD REVISION — 2026-10-07 (appended, not retro-edited)

The conclusion "It did not work" overstated what the record can support. The evidence establishes: the author relied on the tool while preparing, failed the exam three times, and cannot separate the tool's contribution from the author's own preparation method. The amended claim: the author mistook a tool for a teacher, and the failure's ownership is shared and unquantified. The lessons section (Tools Are Not Teachers; Deletion Is Not Documentation) stands unchanged — it is the part this paper got right. Original text stands as published.
